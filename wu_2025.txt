Abstract. Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) are increasingly used in civilian and
military applications, where accurate altitude estimation is critical for safe and reliable
flight. In GPS-denied indoor environments, single sensors such as barometers or laser
rangefinders often suffer from noise, drift, and sudden measurement faults, limiting their
reliability. This paper proposes a multi-sensor altitude fusion algorithm based on an
adaptive Kalman filter that integrates data from a barometer, laser rangefinder, and
accelerometer. A barometer bias term is included in the state-space model for continuous
calibration, while a residual-driven adaptive mechanism dynamically tunes process and
measurement noise to suppress laser anomalies. Simulation results show that the
proposed method improves altitude estimation accuracy and maintains robustness
during sensor disturbances, making it suitable for UAV indoor navigation and control
tasks.
1. Introduction
In recent years, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have been widely applied in fields such as agriculture,
logistics, aerial photography, environmental monitoring, and scientific research. Accurate altitude
measurement is essential for safe flight, as it directly affects navigation, positioning, and control
performance. For outdoor UAVs, common sensors include the Global Navigation Satellite System
(GNSS, e.g., GPS or BDS), inertial measurement units (IMU), and barometers. While GNSS offers high
accuracy at low cost, it becomes unavailable in indoor environments. Barometers, though simple, suffer
from noise and long-term drift, whereas IMUs provide precise short-term estimates but accumulate
errors over time. Indoor UAV operation is particularly challenging due to the absence of GNSS signals,
limited space, and complex interference, which make single-sensor solutions unreliable. To overcome
these limitations, multi-sensor fusion has been increasingly adopted to combine complementary
measurements from different sensors, improving altitude estimation accuracy, robustness, and reliability
[3,4]
. However, traditional Kalman filter–based fusion methods [5] often assume stable measurement
conditions and lack adaptive fault-tolerant mechanisms.
Pritzl et al. proposed an adaptive multi-sensor fusion approach to address the challenges of UAV
altitude estimation in complex indoor environments, including insufficient geometric features, uneven
ground, and sensor measurement delays [1]. Their method fuses measurements from a 1D laser
rangefinder, a 3D LiDAR SLAM algorithm, a barometer, an IMU, and the UAV’s position controller.
Simulation results demonstrated that the approach maintains high robustness and accuracy even under
degraded or delayed measurements, supporting stable UAV flight in complex indoor scenarios. Geng
and Chu Lin developed a navigation scheme based on multi-height sensor fusion and fault-tolerant
Kalman filtering (FTKF) [2]. They fused data from barometers, radar altimeters, and laser rangefinders,

and achieved accurate altitude estimation and stable altitude control.
Therefore, Kalman filter–based multi-sensor altitude fusion is an effective way to improve UAV
altitude-hold performance. Since GPS is unavailable indoors, this paper focuses on fusing barometer,
laser rangefinder, and accelerometer data. To address sudden laser disturbances and slow barometer drift,
a fault-tolerant adaptive Kalman filter is designed with an augmented state model that introduces a
barometric bias term for continuous calibration. Through adaptive noise covariance adjustment, the
method maintains stability and accuracy even during laser anomalies.
2. Multi-Sensor Observation Mode
Due to the lack of GPS signals in indoor environments, this study focuses on alternative sensors such as
barometers, laser rangefinders, and accelerometers for altitude estimation, without relying on GPS.
Moreover, this paper does not actually conduct unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) flights. Instead, it studies
the highly integrated algorithm of UAVs indoors in MATLAB.

4. Simulation Results and Analysis
4.1 Simulation Environment and Parameter Settings
To verify the effectiveness of the proposed adaptive Kalman filter–based multi-sensor altitude fusion
and fault-tolerant method, a MATLAB simulation platform was established. The true UAV altitude was
set to a constant value of 15m . The sampling period was T = 0.1s , and the total number of simulation
steps was N = 6000 . The barometer measurement was modeled as the ideal pressure with added zeromean Gaussian noise (  np = 1 Pa) and a linear drift increasing from 0 to 3 Pa over the simulation. The
laser rangefinder measurements were modeled as Gaussian, with a mean equal to the true altitude and
standard deviation laser = 0.01m .To simulate the situation where an obstacle appears below the UAV,
a disturbance was introduced to the laser rangefinder measurements during t = 300 to 400 s. The initial
filter state was set to h = 14.5m , v = 0 , a = 0 , and e = 0 .

4.5 Analysis and Discussion
The simulation results confirm that the proposed adaptive Kalman filter–based multi-sensor height
fusion and fault-tolerant method achieves robust and stable altitude estimation for indoor UAVs. By
introducing a barometer bias state, the filter continuously compensates for barometric drift, preventing
long-term offset in the fused altitude and validating the effectiveness of the state-augmented model.
Under normal conditions, the estimated altitude closely follows the true value, balancing barometer and
laser data to maintain both accuracy and smoothness.
During laser measurement anomalies, the adaptive mechanism dynamically increases the laser
measurement noise covariance, reducing the influence of faulty data and allowing the estimate to
converge back toward the barometer trend. This behavior is observed for both step and ramp
disturbances, indicating good tolerance to abrupt and gradual errors. Analysis of the Kalman gain,
residuals, and noise covariances shows that the filter adaptively re-weights sensor contributions in
response to abnormal observations, maintaining robustness in multi-sensor environments.
However, as the process noise covariance converges to smaller values, the system becomes more
prediction-driven, which may slightly degrade instantaneous tracking but improves overall stability—
an advantage in indoor flight scenarios.
In recent years, machine learning has been increasingly applied to multi-sensor fusion and anomaly
detection. Unsupervised learning can detect sudden laser or barometer anomalies early and adjust filter
weights more rapidly, while deep learning can model sensor noise characteristics to improve filter
accuracy and robustness. Future work will further explore these methods to enhance adaptability in
complex indoor environments.

5 Conclusion
This paper proposes a multi-sensor altitude fusion and fault-tolerant method for indoor UAVs based on
adaptive Kalman filtering. By introducing a barometer bias state and adaptively adjusting process and
measurement noise covariances, the method continuously calibrates barometer drift and suppresses
sudden laser errors. Simulation results show that:
1. The estimated altitude remains stable under slow barometer drift, eliminating cumulative bias.
2. During step or ramp anomalies in laser measurements, dynamic noise adjustment mitigates
abnormal data and maintains smooth altitude estimation.
3. The adaptive variation of Kalman gain and noise covariances ensures robustness under varying
measurement conditions.
In summary, the proposed method achieves accurate and fault-tolerant altitude estimation in
complex indoor environments, but its response to anomalies remains limited. Future work will explore
machine learning for faster fault detection and more precise adaptive tuning of Kalman filter parameters.
