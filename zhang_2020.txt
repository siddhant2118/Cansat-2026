Improved Kalman Algorithm for Vertical Height and Velocity
Information Fusion of Multi-rotor Unmanned Aerial Vehicle
Xin Zhang
School of Electrical and Information Technology, Changchun Institute of Technology, Changchun 130012
E-mail: 26290256@qq.com
Abstract: The paper presents an improved Kalman filter algorithm. It can ensure the precision and stability of the vertical height
and velocity information of the multi-rotor unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV), when the statistical characteristics of the system
noise and the measurement noise are unknown. The algorithm uses the accelerometer to provide prediction information and
adopts the barometric altimeter to provide measurement information. It can estimates the system noise and the measurement
noise based on the characteristics of these two sensors. The flight experiment and analysis shows that the obtained vertical height
information error is less than 1m, the information update frequency can reach 100Hz, and the good fusion effect of vertical
velocity information is obtained. The improved Kalman algorithm can meet the needs of the multi-rotor unmanned aerial vehicle
flight control.
Key Words: Kalman filter, Information fusion, Multi-rotor unmanned aerial vehicle, Vertical height and velocity estimation

Introduction

Obtaining high-precision, high-update frequency vertical
motion information is the key technology to realize the
autonomous take-off and landing of the multi-rotor
unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV), and is crucial to the
stability and safety of low-height flight [1,2].

The accelerometer calculating process of vertical velocity
and height has a high update frequency and accuracy in a
short time. But it is an integration process and is sensitive to
the vibration of the carrier. Over time, the drift errors
accumulate and become larger. The barometric altimeter has
high accuracy in measuring height information. and there is
no integration process. But the accuracy is lower when the
barometric altimeter is close to the ground, and it is easily
affected by wind disturbances and temperature[3].
Kalman filter is currently recognized as the most suitable
algorithm for the information fusion of the multi-sensor
navigation, but when the conventional Kalman filtering
algorithm is applied, the statistical characteristics of the
system noise and the measurement noise need to be known
[4,5]. However, in the practical applications of the
multi-rotor unmanned aerial vehicle, the statistical
characteristics of the system noise and the measurement
noise are unknown, which makes the conventional Kalman
filter algorithm loses its optimality, the estimation accuracy
is greatly reduced, even will cause the filter divergence. This
paper presents an improved Kalman filter algorithm. It uses

Experiment and analysis

4.1 Experiment Conditions
The experimental data are acquired from the multi-rotor
unmanned aerial vehicle carrying the ADIS16488 inertial
measurement unit (IMU). The inertial measurement unit has
a three-axis accelerometer and a barometric altimeter. The
initial sensitivity of the accelerometer is (1.221 h 10-8)
g/LSB, and the initial sensitivity of the barometric altimeter
is (6.1h10-7) mbar/LSB.

z The accelerometer output information is updated once
every TA Ĭ 0.01s. and the barometric altimeter is
updated once every TB= 0.02s. Every TK=0.02s, The
improved Kalman filter algorithm is used to estimate
vertical height and velocity information.
z Using the information calculated by the accelerometer is
to interpolate between each TK cycle, the frequency of
information fusion can reach f h 100 Hz .
z Evaluation method for the effectiveness of the algorithm:
In flight experiments, the barometric altimeter has high
accuracy when it is far from the ground. Therefore, when
the multi-rotor unmanned aerial vehicle is flying stably
in the air, the barometric altimeter output can be used as
a standard reference curve.

Through analysis, it can be known that the body vibration
of the multi-rotor unmanned aerial vehicle is the main
source of disturbance for navigation information
measurement [7]. The main frequency of body vibration is
about 35Hz, and the output update frequency of the
barometric altimeter is 50Hz, their frequency are very close.
So there is a large error in the measurement noise estimation
value calculated by Formula (12), and the statistical
characteristics of measurement noise cannot be truly
expressed. Therefore, the measurement noise estimation is
